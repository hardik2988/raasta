1. Problem statement

New students and visitors at the UPES Bidholi campus cannot easily work out how to walk from one block to another.

What goes wrong

  • Blocks are named by number (Block 1 to Block 11), so the name gives no clue about location.
  • Search apps show the campus as one pin with partly wrong labels, so they cannot give walking routes between blocks.
  • Asking other people works, but gives different answers every time.

Design goal

Let someone go from "I am here" to "I know the way" in two taps, using a map that is simple enough to read at a glance on a phone.

Success looks like: a first-time user finds a route without any instructions.

2. Target audience

First-year student

Primary

Has a timetable full of block numbers and no mental map yet. Uses a phone, usually between classes with little time.

Visitor or parent

Secondary

Arrives at the gate and needs a hostel or the Management Development Centre. Does not know any block names.

Event volunteer

Secondary

Guides other people and needs directions they can say out loud with confidence.

6. Final design decisions

DecisionReason
A simplified, redrawn map instead of embedding a live mapA live map cannot be restyled. Owning the map means every colour, label and grouping can be a deliberate Gestalt choice.
Labels written by hand from local knowledgeThe online map labelled some places wrongly. Using the real names makes the content trustworthy.
Eight place types, each with colour, shape and letterShape and letters back up colour, so the map still works for people who cannot tell colours apart.
Green-tinted paper, navy ink, one coral accentNeutral surroundings sit quietly beside the green map, and the coral is reserved for the route so it is always the first thing seen.
Navy ink instead of pure blackSofter on screen but still well above the contrast needed for text.
Routes computed from a road graph, not typed inAny of the 21 places can be a start or destination (420 combinations) and every one returns a route.
Steps are tickable and the progress ring closesGives a clear sense of progress and an end point.
Reduced-motion support and keyboard accessAnimations stop for users who prefer less motion, and every control works with a keyboard.
Popular route shortcut chipsFirst-year students and visitors frequently travel between key hotspots and need single-tap route calculation without searching.
Nearest-place finder by categoryStudents with urgent needs (like finding food or sports near their current block) can discover and route to the closest spot immediately.
Dedicated Places directory pageNewcomers need a scannable index of all 21 campus locations with alias search to explore buildings and launch routes from either end.
Place detail modal panel on map pinsTapping a map marker gives students rich context (category, nearest neighbours, and quick A/B assignment) with clear figure-ground separation.
Direction turn line-icons on route stepsPedestrians scanning directions under sunlight or while walking need immediate directional shape recognition (left, right, straight, arrive) rather than reading text alone.
Restrained motion with full reduced-motion supportStaggered load transitions, a single logo ring closure demonstration, and animated number counting give life to the interface while strictly disabling all motion when prefers-reduced-motion is detected.

7. Design system

A modular token system built on an 8px grid, a restrained palette of navy ink, green-tinted paper, and a single coral accent, and an airport-grade icon hierarchy.

7.1 Colour palette & measured contrast

Every colour pair was measured against WCAG 2.1 AA specifications (minimum 4.5:1 for body text, 3.0:1 for large text and UI components). No second accent colour is used; the coral is strictly reserved for the walking route, destination badges, and focal calls-to-action.

7.2 Typography

Raasta pairs Outfit for expressive geometric headings with Inter for high-legibility interface copy. Only three font weights are permitted across the codebase (400 regular, 500 medium, 700 bold) to maintain visual restraint. Headings use tight negative tracking (-0.025em) to form compact word units, line lengths are capped at 60 to 75 characters for comfortable reading, and all times, distances, and step counts utilize tabular numerals (tabular-nums) to prevent jitter during live updates.

7.3 Spacing & Depth

Layout rhythm strictly adheres to an 8px grid scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96px). Elevation relies on a two-level ambient soft shadow system complemented by a 1px inner highlight (inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.85)) rather than harsh drop shadows or neon glows, giving cards a tactile paper feel.

7.4 Iconography & Airport Signage

All user interface and turn-by-turn direction icons are built on a 24px grid with a uniform 2px stroke width, round caps, and round joins. Directional arrows (start, straight, turn left, turn right, sharp left, sharp right, arrive) guide pedestrians like airport terminal wayfinding, pairing an icon badge with concise instructions and right-aligned distances.

Known limits, stated openly: the map is schematic, not surveyed; walking times assume about 80 metres per minute; road names are descriptive labels for this project.
